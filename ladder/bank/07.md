# 7 · Merging: move everything, forget nothing

**The idea.** A merge is where hidden state bites. Your account now lives in
FOUR places: its balance, its outgoing total, its records, and any pending
payments that point at its id. Merging means visiting every one of them.
Miss one and the bug shows up days later, somewhere else.

The habit: before writing a merge (or a delete, or a rename), list every
place that mentions the id. Here is a school merging two classes:

```python
def merge_classes(school, keep, gone):
    school["size"][keep] += school["size"].pop(gone)     # pop: remove AND return
    both = school["marks"][keep] + school["marks"].pop(gone)
    school["marks"][keep] = sorted(both, key=lambda m: m[0])   # by date
    for trip in school["trips"]:
        if trip["class"] == gone:
            trip["class"] = keep
```

`dict.pop(key)` removes the key and hands you its value in one move, so
nothing is left behind by accident.

**Your turn.** `merge_accounts(ts, id1, id2)` → `True`, or `False` if the
ids are the same or either is unknown. On success:
- id1 gets id2's balance and outgoing total added to its own
- id1's history becomes both histories together, sorted by timestamp
- id2's pending payments now belong to id1 (id1 can cancel them, they are
  paid from id1)
- id2 no longer exists: every method treats it as unknown, and
  `create_account` can make a fresh, empty `id2` again

**Checked:** balance, history, balance_at, outgoing, moved payments run
from id1 and can only be cancelled by id1, id2 truly gone and reusable.
